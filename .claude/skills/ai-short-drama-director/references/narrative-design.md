## §1 Hook Formulas（開場鉤子公式）

The hook must land in the **first 3 s**. On vertical platforms the first frame is the thumbnail, so it must already ask a question.

| # | Type | Structure | Formula | AI short-drama adaptation | Dark fairy-tale seed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 誤會／資訊差 Misunderstanding | setup → information revealed → reversal/truth | Audience thinks X, it is actually Y | Manage *when* information is disclosed; build tension where the audience knows more than the character | A lullaby-singing grandmother, but her shadow has no hands. |
| 2 | 身分反差 Identity contrast | build expectation → identity reveal → contrast shock | Looks like X, is actually Y | Ceremonial entrance for the reveal; a backlit silhouette hides identity until the turn | A tiny doll-like girl who turns out to be the forest's keeper of the dead. |
| 3 | 時間壓力 Time pressure | set a limit → countdown → resolved/failed | X must happen before T | Establish the limit with an object (never on-screen text): a clock, a melting candle, the moon crossing a window | Get home before the last candle in the lantern dies. |
| 4 | 情緒累積 Emotional accumulation | daily state → detail build-up → outburst | Details replace statements; the audience feels it themselves | Environmental details replace lines | A place set at the table for someone, dusted every day for years. |
| 5 | 規則禁忌 Forbidden rule *(extension)* | a rule is shown → someone breaks it → consequence | "Never do X" → X is done | Show the rule as an object (a carved warning, a nailed-shut door, a covered mirror) in shot 1 | Every mirror in the house is covered, and a child lifts one cloth. |
| 6 | 冷開場倒敘 Cold open *(extension)* | most striking end frame → rewind → road back | Show the result first, withhold the cause | Clip 1 opens on the climax image, then `continuity_out` jumps back in time | A girl standing in a burning orchard, calm — then "earlier that night". |

## §2 Pacing & Reversal Density（節奏與反轉密度）

**Turning points (for pieces of 3 min or longer):** establish the core conflict or suspense hook in minute 1, escalate or introduce a new element in minute 2, and reach the first climax or major turn in minute 3.

**Scaled rule (for shorter pieces):** hook ≤ 3 s → conflict established by **1/3** → climax by **3/4** → final beat or aftertaste in the last 10–15%.

**Reversal density.** The source rule gives **reversal interval ≈ total ÷ 4** (60 s → one every 15 s; 180 s → one every 45 s). Very short pieces are capped:

| Total | Minimum reversals | Suggested positions |
|---|---|---|
| 15 s | 1 | ~10–13 s |
| 30 s | 1–2 | ~18 s, ~26 s |
| 45 s | 2–3 | ~20 s, ~32 s, ~40 s |
| 60 s | 4 | ~15 / 30 / 45 / 55 s |
| 90 s | 4 | every ~22 s |
| 180 s | 4+ | every ~45 s |

A reversal is a change in what the audience believes, not just a new event. The last reversal should reframe the first frame.

## §3 Director Brief Template（導演意圖 Dir. Brief）

Fill internally and compress into the output's 🎬 導演意圖 section.

- **Basic:** title · episode/scene · target duration · ratio · target model (if given)
- **Narrative target:** core event · audience emotional target (tense / sad / unexpected / warm / dread…) · emotion curve (foreshadow → accumulate → burst / fall back) · information split (what the audience knows vs. what the character knows)
- **Visual target:** tone (cold / dreamlike / real…) · main colours (hue + value range, hex) · core camera language ("fixed gaze", "slow push", "handheld pursuit") · forbidden list ("any sunny warm tone", "front light")
- **Sound target:** ambience ("rain + distant thunder", "library silence") · score direction for post ("low strings, no melody", "detuned music box", "no music") · sound–image relation (sync / counterpoint / delayed reveal) · key sound moment ("a scream before the door opens")
- **Rhythm target:** rhythm pattern (fast cut / slow push / still + burst / breathing) · shot density · rhythm marks
- **Special:** prop anchors · AI generation keys ("backlit silhouette", "reflection") · self-check points

## §4 Director's Three Questions（導演三問）

1. **What does the audience know that the character does not?** This covers suspense, information asymmetry and the cognitive gap. It decides shot placement and editing order.
2. **What emotion should the audience feel here?** This covers the emotional target and the visual/sound means. It decides shot size, rhythm, light, colour and sound.
3. **Where does this scene's "cinema" come from?** This covers what only film can do, as opposed to prose or stage. It decides camera movement, composition, light design and sound design.

## §5 Narrative Templates（敘事模板庫）

### A — Restrained Tragedy（克制悲劇）
Use for major emotional events without melodrama. Core: the heavier the emotion, the lighter the expression.
- **Setup:** write the state, not the feeling. *He sits by the window. The tea is cold. He does not move.*
- **Trigger:** handle the event minimally. *The phone rings. He glances at it. He doesn't answer.*
- **Action:** carry the emotion through a daily act. *He stands, pours out the tea — slowly, like a ritual.*
- **Close:** end at the edge of a feeling. *After that day he never drank tea again.* (Render as an image of the unused cup, not as narration.)

### B — Release Scene（釋放場景）
Use after a major turn, when the character releases emotion. Core: emotional progression = space opening × light change × movement progression.
1. **Transition:** closed space → open space; dim → natural light; background sound rises.
2. **Action:** iconic act (running, falling, looking up); medium follow shot; speed goes fast → slow.
3. **Sublimation:** low angle or wide shot; character within the environment; warmer tones.
4. **Internalization:** close-up on eyes, lips or hands; stillness or extreme slow motion; ambience drops out, leaving only breath.

### C — Suspense Formula（懸疑公式）
Core: suspense = audience knows + character doesn't + time-limit threat.
1. Show the threat first, from the audience's view.
2. Cut to the character behaving normally, unaware.
3. Add a time element (countdown, a door about to open).
4. Cut away just before discovery.
5. Use sound cues: a clock, footsteps, breathing.

*Example:* A night-shift clerk stocks shelves. Through the glass door the audience sees a shadow approach one step every 5 s. The auto-door is about to open, and the music stays bright. The audience knows something is wrong.

### D — Occluded Narrative（遮蔽敘事）
Core: hiding beats showing; the audience's imagination fills the gap.
- **Occlusion methods:**
  - Sound: a scream with nothing seen.
  - Partial: only a body part is shown.
  - Light: darkness or fog blurs the subject.
  - Angle: one corner is never shown.
  - Time: the key event happens off-screen.
- **Priority:** unfinished > complete; a gasp > the scary image; an approaching shadow > the person arriving.

*Example (a photo in the park):* front view → blurred occluded wide → a gasp → someone packing up → a shadow approaching → the photo is revealed, but the face stays hidden.

### E — Circular Narrative（循環敘事）
Core: break linear time; the ending closes into the opening. Built on reversible causality, folded time and puzzle structure.
1. The protagonist is trapped in a loop.
2. Each round yields new information.
3. Key details change between rounds.
4. The ending reveals that the loop's cause comes from another timeline.

*AI adaptation:* keep the scene identical and change prop details (a photo date, a wall poster, the weather). Jump cuts mark time jumps. Superimpose memory over reality.

### F — Restricted Space（受限空間）
Core: the story stays inside a few spaces, and the space itself is the pressure cooker. Enclosure amplifies tension, cutting off exits leaves only internal conflict, fewer distractions keep the focus on the characters, and fewer escape options raise the stakes.
*Classic settings:* the back seat of a late-night taxi (reunion → old grudge → secret → trust collapses); an elevator (a power game between boss and subordinate); an office during a blackout (the true self emerges once daily order breaks).

### G — Fairy-Tale Inversion（童話變奏）*(extension, default-style native)*
Core: take a fairy-tale rule the audience already trusts, then invert who is the monster.
1. Quote the familiar setup visually (a red hood, a gingerbread house, a glass slipper).
2. Let the audience's genre expectation run.
3. Plant one wrong detail early, using the foreground suspense method.
4. Invert: the victim is the predator, or the rescue is the trap.
5. Close on an image that makes the opening frame read differently.

## §6 Hook × Template Pairing（搭配建議）

| Template | Best hooks | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A Restrained tragedy | 4 Accumulation, 6 Cold open | Details carry the grief; a cold open lets the aftermath lead |
| B Release | 3 Time pressure, 4 Accumulation | Pressure builds the need for release |
| C Suspense | 3 Time pressure, 1 Misunderstanding | Needs a clock and an information gap |
| D Occluded | 1 Misunderstanding, 5 Forbidden rule | The unseen thing is the rule-breaker |
| E Circular | 6 Cold open, 1 Misunderstanding | The end frame doubles as the first frame |
| F Restricted space | 2 Identity contrast, 3 Time pressure | A small room plus a hidden identity equals pressure |
| G Fairy-tale inversion | 2 Identity contrast, 5 Forbidden rule | Genre trust is the setup; the identity flip is the payoff |

## §7 Narrative Thinking Tools（敘事思維技巧）

- **Suspense formula:** suspense = audience knows + character doesn't + time threat. Use it to sustain tension rather than to shock; information asymmetry is the core weapon.
- **Flashback anchor:** every flashback is triggered by a concrete prop, sound, smell, touch or word, and the character actively recalls it. Use it for trauma, emotional threads and suspense reveals. *In video:* match-cut from the anchor object to the same object in the past (see `shot-craft-library.md` §6 Transitions).
- **Subtext:** what the line says ≠ what the character thinks. Line tension = the gap between text and subtext. Use it for power struggles, confessions and family conflict.
- **Image superposition:** see `shot-craft-library.md` §11.

## §8 Dialogue Staging Preferences（對白場面調度偏好）

Use these even when there are no spoken lines — they stage any two-person exchange.

| Preference | Effect | Prompt phrase |
|---|---|---|
| Two-shot, no cutting | The audience reads the relationship itself | `static two-shot, both in frame, no cuts` |
| Film the listener, not the speaker | A line's weight lands through the reaction | `hold on listener's face, speaker off-screen` |
| Side-by-side parallel composition | Intimacy or avoidance without confrontation | `profile two-shot, side by side, both facing same direction` |
| Back view / back of head | The inner state stays hidden | `from behind, back of head in foreground` |
| Spatial interruption | Empty environmental cutaways break up the exchange | `cutaway to empty corridor / ticking clock` |
| Mirror / reflection | What we see is not the "real" person | `seen only in mirror reflection` |
| Long take with blocking | Movement replaces editing | `single continuous take, characters cross and re-frame` |

**Writing narrative purpose.** Name the audio-visual means, the motif if any, and "what + why" in one objective sentence.
- ✔ "Back-to-back composition shows the rift between them and sets up the coming conflict."
- ✔ "The spectacle scales one person's act into a crowd phenomenon — a metaphor for how cities copy themselves."
- ✘ "Shows the character's inner conflict" (does not say how). ✘ "Advances the plot" (every shot does). ✘ "Expresses the theme" (the theme is a result, not a purpose).

## §9 Dialogue Policy（台詞政策）

- **Default:** no spoken lines. Tell the story with image plus non-verbal sound.
- **Only when the user supplies lines or asks for dialogue or narration:**
  - Keep the user's wording and language verbatim, and assign each line to its shot.
  - If the user asked you to write the lines: use subtext, keep them short, avoid on-the-nose statements and "as you know" exposition, and treat silence as a line.
  - Short lines are explosive and fragmented. Long lines carry philosophical weight about life, people or society, never banal narration.
  - **Japanese: NO KANJI.** Hiragana and katakana only.
- **Subtitles and on-screen text** never go into a prompt; list them in 後製備註 with timecodes.
